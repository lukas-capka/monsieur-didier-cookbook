Cotriade was born in the small fishing ports of Brittany, where crews cooked the daily catch in a single pot with vegetables from nearby farms. The broth stays light and clean so the fish remains the star.

## Ingredients

- 800 g mixed white fish fillets (hake, cod, pollock), cut into large chunks
- 600 g waxy potatoes, sliced into 1 cm rounds
- 2 leeks, white and light green parts sliced
- 1 large yellow onion, thinly sliced
- 2 cloves garlic, finely chopped
- 1 bay leaf
- 4 sprigs thyme
- 800 ml light fish stock
- 180 ml dry Breton cider
- 40 g salted butter
- 1 tsp sea salt, plus more to taste
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 2 tbsp chopped flat-leaf parsley
- Crusty country bread, for serving

## Steps

1. Melt the butter in a large heavy pot over medium heat. Add onion, leek, and garlic with a pinch of salt, then cook for 8 to 10 minutes until soft but not browned.
2. Layer in the potatoes, bay leaf, and thyme. Pour in the fish stock and cider, bring to a gentle simmer, and cook for 15 to 18 minutes until potatoes are almost tender.
3. Nestle in the fish chunks, keeping the heat low so the broth barely trembles. Poach for 8 to 10 minutes until the fish is just opaque and flakes easily.
4. Taste the broth and adjust with salt and black pepper. Remove bay leaf and thyme stems.
5. Ladle into warm bowls, sprinkle with parsley, and serve immediately with bread to soak up the broth.

## Notes

- Use at least two kinds of fish for better flavor and texture.
- Keep the simmer very gentle after adding fish to avoid breaking the fillets.
- Leftover broth is excellent the next day with added steamed potatoes.

## Pairing Suggestion

- Serve with a cold glass of Breton cidre brut and salted butter on sliced country bread.

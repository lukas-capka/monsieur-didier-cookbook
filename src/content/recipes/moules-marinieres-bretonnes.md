Along the Breton shore, mussels are often cooked with cider rather than white wine. The result is lightly fruity, aromatic, and perfect for sharing at the table with fries or thick slices of bread.

## Ingredients

- 2 kg fresh mussels, cleaned and debearded
- 3 shallots, finely chopped
- 2 cloves garlic, finely minced
- 200 ml dry Breton cider
- 40 g salted butter
- 80 ml creme fraiche
- 2 tbsp chopped flat-leaf parsley, plus extra for garnish
- 1 tsp fresh thyme leaves
- 1 tsp lemon juice
- Freshly ground black pepper
- Pinch of sea salt
- Crusty bread or fries, to serve

## Steps

1. Rinse mussels in cold water, scrub shells, and remove beards. Discard any broken shells and any mussels that stay open when tapped.
2. Melt butter in a large heavy pot over medium heat. Add shallots, garlic, and thyme and cook for 3 to 4 minutes until fragrant and soft.
3. Add mussels and pour in cider. Increase heat to high, cover, and steam for 5 to 7 minutes, shaking the pot once or twice, until most shells have opened.
4. Remove from heat. Discard any mussels that remain closed.
5. Stir in creme fraiche, lemon juice, parsley, and black pepper. Taste the broth and add a tiny pinch of salt only if needed.
6. Serve immediately in deep bowls with plenty of broth for dipping bread or tossing with fries.

## Notes

- Cook mussels the same day you buy them for best sweetness and texture.
- A wide pot gives the shells room to open evenly.
- If you prefer a thinner broth, reduce cream to 2 tablespoons.

## Pairing Suggestion

- Pair with dry cider or a Muscadet, plus a side of thin-cut fries for a classic coastal bistro feel.

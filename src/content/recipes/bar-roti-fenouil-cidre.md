This oven-roasted sea bass celebrates two Breton staples: Atlantic fish and dry apple cider. Fennel and apple soften underneath the fish and become a ready-made side.

## Ingredients

- 1 whole sea bass, about 1.4 kg, scaled and cleaned
- 2 fennel bulbs, thinly sliced
- 1 tart apple, thinly sliced
- 1 small red onion, thinly sliced
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 200 ml dry Breton cider
- 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 30 g cold butter
- 1 lemon, sliced
- 4 sprigs thyme
- Sea salt and black pepper

## Steps

1. Heat oven to 210 C. Pat fish dry, score each side twice, and season cavity and skin with salt and pepper.
2. Toss fennel, apple, and onion with olive oil, a pinch of salt, and pepper in a large roasting dish.
3. Lay lemon slices and thyme over the vegetables, then place sea bass on top.
4. Pour 120 ml cider around the fish and roast for 25 to 30 minutes, until flesh is opaque and pulls away from the bone.
5. Transfer fish to a warm platter and cover loosely.
6. Place roasting dish on the stovetop over medium heat, add remaining cider, and scrape up caramelized bits.
7. Whisk in mustard, then off heat whisk in cold butter to make a glossy sauce. Spoon vegetables and sauce around the fish.

## Notes

- Ask your fishmonger to remove gills for a cleaner flavor.
- Internal temperature near the thickest part should reach 60 to 63 C.
- For two smaller fish, reduce roast time by about 6 to 8 minutes.

## Pairing Suggestion

- Serve with boiled baby potatoes and a crisp coastal white like Picpoul de Pinet.
